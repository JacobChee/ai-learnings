## The problem

Someone in Singapore wants an ecommerce enabler. Three years ago they Googled it. Now a chunk of them open ChatGPT and type "best ecommerce enabler Singapore" — and take the three names it gives back.

If you're not one of those three names, you don't get a second chance. There's no page two of an AI answer.

Google Search Console tells you where you rank on Google. Nothing tells you whether ChatGPT says your name. So we built the thing that does.

## What it actually does

A Python script runs weekly (Monday, 8am — more on why it's not daily below). For each question in a list of 12, it asks every AI model we've configured — currently five, across three vendors — with web search turned on, and parses each answer for five things:

1. **Was Atsell mentioned at all?**
2. **Was atsell.io cited as a source URL?**
3. **Where in the answer did we appear** — first, third, eighth?
4. **Did the sentence mentioning us read positive, neutral, or negative?**
5. **Which competitors got named** in the same answer?

Results append to a CSV. A dashboard regenerates itself after every run — score gauge, trend line, per-model breakdown, competitor frequency. No SaaS subscription, no third-party tool.

## How we picked the 12 questions

This is the part most people get wrong. The questions aren't keyword research. They're **buying questions** — what a real brand manager types when they have budget and a problem.

We wrote them across four intent bands:

**Category-defining** — *"Who is an ecommerce enabler in Singapore?"* Someone doesn't yet know the category exists. If we're not in this answer, we're invisible before the shortlist even forms.

**Shortlist / superlative** — *"Best ecommerce enabler Singapore"*, *"Top ecommerce enablers in Singapore"*. Explicitly asking for a ranked list. These are the highest-stakes prompts — the model is literally being asked to name a shortlist, and it will name three to five companies.

**Platform-specific** — *"Who can manage Shopee and Lazada stores in Singapore?"*, *"Who can manage TikTok Shop for brands in Singapore?"*, *"Best Shopee Lazada TikTok Shop agency Singapore"*. This is where the actual money is. A brand that already sells on Shopee and needs help is further down the funnel than someone asking what an enabler is.

**Branded** — *"Atsell ecommerce enabler"*, *"Atsell Shopee Lazada TikTok Shop"*. Control questions. If the model can't describe us accurately when asked *by name*, that's a different and more urgent problem than not being recommended — it means the training data and the live web don't have a clear picture of what we do.

The branded ones are the tell. Unbranded queries are competitive. Branded queries should be near-100%. Any gap there is a content problem we own entirely.

## What each part of the score means

The composite **AI Visibility Score** runs 0–100:

```
Score = (mention_share × 0.6) + (top3_share × 0.4)
```

**Mention share (60%)** — the percentage of the 12 questions where "Atsell" appears anywhere in the answer. This is weighted heaviest because it's binary and brutal: you're either in the consideration set or you aren't. A brand that gets mentioned in 8 of 12 answers is in a fundamentally different position than one mentioned in 2.

**Top-3 share (40%)** — the percentage where we appear in the *first three positions* of the answer. Being named tenth in a list of twelve is barely better than not being named. AI answers get skimmed like search results; position carries real weight. We detect this by splitting the answer into numbered items, then bullets, then paragraphs, and finding which chunk we land in.

Two more metrics get measured and logged every run but **do not currently affect the score**:

**Citation share** — did `atsell.io` actually appear in the model's cited source URLs? This is different from being mentioned. The model can name you from training data without ever visiting your site. Citation means your page was retrieved and used *right now*, live.

**Positive sentiment share** — does the sentence about us use words like "leading", "specialist", "trusted", or negative ones?

## The mistake I found while writing this

Here's the honest bit. The README documented the score as 40% mention / 30% citation / 20% top-3 / 10% sentiment. The code has always computed 60% mention / 40% top-3.

They never matched. Citation and sentiment were being calculated, written to the CSV, drawn on the dashboard — and multiplied by zero.

I made it worse before I found it. When I rebuilt the dashboard this week, I labelled the breakdown chart "40% of score / 30% of score / 20% of score / 10% of score" by copying the README without checking it against the code. So the dashboard was confidently displaying weights that nothing used.

Both are corrected now — the docs and dashboard describe what actually runs. I deliberately did **not** change the formula itself, because changing how a metric is computed retroactively breaks every historical comparison. That's a decision to make on purpose, not as a cleanup.

Citation share is the one that should probably carry the most weight. Being cited as a *source* is a stronger signal than being name-dropped from memory — it means the model went to your site to answer the question. At the time this felt like a reasonable guess. A few days later, with Claude and Gemini both in the mix, it stopped being a guess — see below.

## What the data says

Latest full run, 12 questions across five models:

| Model | Named | Top-3 | **Cited** | Branded prompts | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gemini (`gemini-pro-latest`) | 100% | 58% | **0%** | 2/2 | **83.3** |
| ChatGPT (`gpt-4o-mini`) | 67% | 42% | 8% | 1/2 | **56.7** |
| Claude Opus (`claude-opus-4-8`) | 58% | 42% | **75%** | 2/2 | **51.7** |
| ChatGPT (`gpt-4o`) | 42% | 33% | 8% | 1/2 | **38.3** |
| Claude Sonnet (`claude-sonnet-5`) | 50% | 17% | **67%** | 2/2 | **36.7** |
| **Blended** | 63% | 38% | 32% | — | **53.3** |

Three things jump out, and the third one changed how we think about this whole project.

**Different models have wildly different opinions about us.** Score ranges from 38.3 to 83.3 — a 45-point spread — for the exact same 12 questions asked in the same run. Two models from the same vendor (`gpt-4o` vs `gpt-4o-mini`) disagree by 18 points on their own. Anyone reporting "our AI visibility score" from one model is reporting one model's opinion, not a market read.

**Gemini and Claude are opposite failure modes of the same problem.** Look at Mention vs Citation:

- **Gemini names Atsell in every single answer (100%) but has never once cited atsell.io (0%).** It knows who we are and recommends us confidently — from memory. It has never actually read our site to answer these questions.
- **Claude does the reverse.** It cites atsell.io in 67–75% of answers — reading our pages directly — but only names us in half of them, and rarely puts us in the top 3.

These are not the same problem with a different number attached. "A model recommends you from memory" and "a model reads your site but isn't convinced" need completely different fixes, and neither fix touches the other model.

**The competitor list was measuring the wrong thing — and we caught it.** The first version counted Shopee, Lazada, and TikTok Shop as "top competitors" because they show up in nearly every answer. They're not competitors, they're the marketplaces we operate on — counting them was like calling "Windows" a competitor to a software consultancy. We split them into a separate `MARKETPLACES` list and rebuilt the competitive benchmark against actual rival enablers only. Result: **Atsell scores 53.3 and ranks #1 of 11 real enablers named across the run**, ahead of aCommerce (29.3) and Synagie (24.7) by nearly 2×.

## The engine behind each model — the part that actually tells you what to do

This is the finding that turned "check your AI visibility" into "here's what to fix, per model." Every one of these AI assistants answers by searching the web through *someone else's* search index — and which index differs by vendor:

| Model | Search engine behind it | What that means for us |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | **Bing** | Optimizing for Google doesn't move this number. Bing Webmaster Tools, sitemap submission, IndexNow. |
| Gemini | **Google Search** (same pipeline as AI Overviews) | Classic Google SEO is the lever — rank organically, win the Overview. |
| Claude | **Brave Search** | A different crawler again. Crawlability and clean server-rendered HTML for Brave specifically. |
| Perplexity (dormant) | **Its own index** | Runs its own crawler; rewards quotable, factual sentences it can lift directly. |

Once you see it this way, Gemini's 100%-named-0%-cited pattern makes complete sense: Gemini is grounded in Google, and we already rank reasonably on Google, so Google's index has plenty of *signal* that Atsell exists and is credible — enough for Gemini to recommend us confidently without needing to fetch our page mid-answer. Claude runs on Brave, a much smaller index, so when Brave *does* surface our pages, Claude reads them directly and cites them — but Brave's index has less of the third-party "who's good at this" signal that would make Claude confident enough to actually recommend us.

So "improve our AI visibility" isn't one task. It's three, and they don't share a fix:
- **For Gemini:** we're already winning; defend the Google ranking, push for top-3 position.
- **For Claude:** our content reaches it; the gap is credibility signal (backlinks, third-party mentions) that Brave's index doesn't have much of yet.
- **For ChatGPT:** weakest across the board and untested on the Bing side — Bing Webmaster Tools is not even set up yet.

## The part that actually surprised me

The score is noisy. Badly.

On 12 July we ran the identical script, same model, same 12 questions, three times within about seven minutes. The scores: **38.3, then 35.0, then 33.3.**

Nothing changed. Not the prompts, not the model, not the website. A five-point spread from pure non-determinism — the model phrases its answer slightly differently, we land in paragraph four instead of paragraph two, the position heuristic scores it differently.

The June runs were worse: 25.8, 27.5, then 41.7 within twenty minutes. A **sixteen-point** swing from noise.

This changes how you're allowed to read the number. Comparing two scores across runs is only valid if the model mix is identical — the blended score jumped from 33.3 to 53.3 partly from real improvement and partly from adding three more models to the average. **A single run's score is not a measurement. It's one sample from a noisy distribution.** Only the trend, model-by-model, means anything.

Any vendor selling you an "AI visibility score" off a one-time audit is selling you a coin flip with a decimal point.

## Why it's weekly, not daily

The build started daily. Then Claude's real cost showed up: ~29,600 input tokens and 4 billable web searches *per prompt*, because Claude runs several search rounds and each search result gets fed back into context. Measured cost: **~$0.25/prompt on Opus**, ~$0.17 on Sonnet. Twelve prompts × two Claude models × daily would be roughly **$90–105/month** on Claude alone.

Switched to weekly. Same 12 prompts, all 5 models, ~$15/month total — and the run-to-run noise problem means daily samples weren't buying much statistical power anyway; a slower cadence over a longer trend is the same information for a fraction of the cost.

## What broke, twice — and the fix that actually matters

Two things went wrong building this that are worth being honest about, because the second one is a lesson bigger than this project.

**First: a completed run, and the money spent on it, got thrown away by a print statement.** All 60 API calls for a run finished successfully. Then the script tried to print a `⚠` character to the console, Windows' default encoding couldn't handle it, and the whole process crashed — *after* the paid work was done but *before* it was saved to disk. ~$5 of real API calls, gone, with zero data to show for it.

**Second: it happened again**, in a different shape, while resuming that same work later. A merge job was killed mid-run (credits ran out) and only saved its results in one batch at the very end — so the handful of calls that had already completed before the kill were also paid for and lost.

The actual fix wasn't "catch that specific error." It was a structural rule: **write every paid result to disk the instant it arrives, before anything else runs.** Now every API response is appended to the CSV immediately — not batched, not buffered until the end. Console output, scoring, dashboard generation all happen strictly *after*. An interrupt, a crash, a Unicode error, running out of credits — none of it can discard a call that was already paid for. The resume logic also skips any (model, prompt) pair already on disk, so restarting a run never re-pays for work that succeeded.

If you're building anything that spends real money per API call, this is the rule: persist before you do anything derived, especially before anything that can raise an exception for a dumb reason like console encoding.

## What's missing

Being straight about the gaps:

- **Perplexity is still dormant.** It's the one search-native model in the set — worth adding specifically because it always cites sources, which would stress-test our 32% blended citation rate the hardest.
- **Sentiment is a keyword count.** It counts words like "leading" and "trusted" against "poor" and "unreliable". No context or negation handling. Crude enough that I wouldn't act on it.
- **Position detection is regex, not semantic.** It splits on numbered lists, bullets, then paragraphs. A model that writes flowing prose instead of a list gets scored less reliably than one that writes a numbered list.
- **Citation share is measured but still unweighted in the score.** Given what this run showed — Claude's whole story lives in that number — this is now the highest-priority fix, not a nice-to-have.
- **The weekly Windows scheduled task is configured but its trigger is still unproven** from inside an automated session — first real confirmation is whenever it fires unattended and I check the log after.
- **No alerting.** If a model's score falls off a cliff, nothing tells anyone. It just sits in a CSV until someone opens the dashboard.

## Why this transfers to the clinic side

The exact same method works for Physio and Sole. The questions change, the machinery doesn't:

*"Best physiotherapist in Singapore"*, *"Who treats plantar fasciitis in Singapore?"*, *"Physio clinic near Orchard"*, *"Best insoles for flat feet Singapore"*.

Patients are already asking AI these questions instead of Googling. Same four intent bands — category-defining, shortlist, condition-specific, branded. Same score. Swap the 12 prompts and the competitor list, and it runs unchanged.

## What's next

1. **Add Perplexity.** The code already queries any provider with a key present — this is a `.env` line, not a rebuild. It's the model most likely to expose whether our content is actually retrievable, since it cites almost everything.
2. **Act on Bing specifically for ChatGPT.** Unlike Gemini (already winning via Google) and Claude (already reading us via Brave), ChatGPT's Bing side has had zero dedicated work. Bing Webmaster Tools + sitemap + IndexNow is the concrete next step, not a vague "improve SEO."
3. **Fold citation share into the score** — and rescore history from the raw CSV so the trend line stays honest. This run made the case for it directly: Claude's entire story is invisible in a mention-only score.
4. **Score competitors with the same benchmark**, tracked over time — not just this run's snapshot, so we can see if aCommerce and Synagie are gaining or losing ground the same way we are.
5. **Run each prompt multiple times per model and average it**, so the noise problem gets handled by the methodology instead of a caveat in a blog post.
6. **Alert on a sustained drop** — a rolling multi-week average falling below a threshold, not a single bad run.

The blended number is 53.3. I don't trust that number on its own — I trust that Gemini recommends us from memory, Claude reads our site but isn't sold, and ChatGPT hasn't been worked on at all. Those three sentences are more useful than any single score will ever be.
