We connected the Afix WhatsApp number to Hermes so incoming customer enquiries can be answered by an AI agent.

This is not using Meta's official WhatsApp Business Cloud API. We used the normal WhatsApp linked-device route — basically like connecting WhatsApp Web by scanning a QR code.

That made setup fast. But the real learning was not just "can AI reply?" The real learning was: **can we make the chat feel clean, useful, and safe enough for actual customers?**

## What we built

The backend flow is:

```text
Customer messages Afix on WhatsApp
        ↓
Afix WhatsApp number receives it
        ↓
Hermes WhatsApp bridge reads the message
        ↓
Hermes AI agent replies using Afix rules/pricing
        ↓
Reply is sent back through WhatsApp
```

We also kept Telegram connected as an operator/admin channel, while WhatsApp is the customer-facing channel.

The bot was given Afix-specific knowledge from afix.sg, including:

- Aircon servicing pricing
- Chemical wash pricing
- Annual care plan pricing
- Carpentry guide prices
- Renovation and build-out service scope
- Booking questions to ask
- What not to quote
- When to hand over to a human

## Why we chose normal WhatsApp first

We deliberately chose the normal WhatsApp bridge route because it is faster for a pilot.

No Meta developer setup. No webhook approval. No template-message rules. Just scan a QR code under:

```text
WhatsApp → Settings → Linked Devices → Link a Device
```

For testing, this is the fastest path.

But it has a clear limitation: the machine running Hermes must stay awake. If the PC sleeps, the bot stops replying. Long term, this should move to either:

1. an always-on office mini PC,
2. a cloud VPS, or
3. the official WhatsApp Business Cloud API.

For a pilot, normal WhatsApp is good enough. For production, we should not pretend it is the final architecture.

## The first big issue: backend messages leaked into customer WhatsApp

The first tests worked, but ugly backend messages appeared in the WhatsApp chat, such as:

```text
No home channel is set for WhatsApp...
Reading skill business-whatsapp-responder
Interrupting current task...
Redirected current run...
Gateway shutting down...
```

These are useful for an operator. They are terrible for customers.

So we learned that a customer-facing AI channel needs a different configuration from an internal AI assistant.

For WhatsApp, we turned off:

- tool progress messages,
- interim assistant messages,
- long-running status messages,
- busy/interrupt acknowledgements,
- gateway restart/shutdown notices,
- the default "Hermes Agent" reply prefix.

The customer should only see the actual Afix reply. Nothing about tools, skills, gateway, iterations, or backend state.

## The second big issue: wording matters more than expected

A customer said something like:

```text
I need to wash 7 units at Orchard
```

The bot assumed "wash" meant chemical wash and quoted:

```text
7 units chemical wash = $50/unit, total $350
```

That was wrong.

For Afix, **normal wash = normal servicing** unless the customer clearly asks for chemical wash or describes a problem that makes chemical wash relevant.

So we added this rule:

> Do not assume "wash" means chemical wash. "Normal wash", "aircon wash", "wash aircon", and "normal servicing" usually mean normal aircon servicing.

Correct response should be:

```text
Normal servicing for 7 units is $25/unit, total $175.
```

Only quote chemical wash if the customer says chemical wash, deep cleaning, leaking, bad smell, mould, not cold, or very dirty units.

This is a good example of why AI bots need business-specific language rules. Customers do not speak in neat service categories.

## Current Afix pricing logic

The bot should quote only published prices.

### Normal aircon servicing / normal wash

| Units | Price |
|---:|---:|
| 1 unit | $50 |
| 2 units | $30/unit |
| 3+ units | $25/unit |

Example:

```text
7 units = 7 × $25 = $175
```

### Chemical wash

| Units | Price |
|---:|---:|
| 1 unit | $60 |
| 2+ units | $50/unit |

Chemical wash should not be assumed from the word "wash" alone.

### Carpentry guide pricing

| Item | Guide price |
|---|---:|
| Kitchen upper cabinet | $132/ft |
| Kitchen base cabinet | $132/ft, excluding countertop |
| Kitchen island | $264/ft |
| Bedroom wardrobe | $264/ft |
| Wall-mounted TV console | $156/ft, minimum 5 ft, from $780 |
| Floor-standing TV console | $180/ft, minimum 5 ft, from $900 |

For renovation, repair parts, complex troubleshooting, and custom works, the bot should collect details and hand over to the team.

## The bot's ideal role

The bot should behave like a front-desk assistant, not a salesperson pretending to know everything.

It can:

- answer basic pricing,
- explain service scope,
- ask for number of units,
- ask for location,
- ask for preferred date/time,
- ask for photos/videos when there is leakage, noise, or damage,
- say the team will confirm the final slot or quote.

It should not:

- confirm final appointment slots,
- promise same-day availability,
- offer discounts,
- invent prices for repair parts,
- handle complaints fully by itself,
- ask for payment details, OTPs, passwords, or sensitive documents,
- continue talking after a human has clearly taken over.

## Human takeover is still an open item

For now, if a human from Afix steps into the WhatsApp chat, the bot may still reply later when the customer sends another message.

That is acceptable for a pilot, but not ideal for production.

The better future logic is:

```text
Human staff replies in customer chat
        ↓
Bot pauses that customer thread for 24 hours
        ↓
Customer replies during pause
        ↓
Bot stays silent
```

This prevents the bot from interrupting a real human conversation.

Recommended rule:

> Once a human intervenes, pause the bot for that customer for 24 hours or until manually resumed.

## Website updates are not automatically reflected yet

The bot's pricing knowledge came from afix.sg, but it does not magically update every time the website changes.

So we added a scheduled crawler:

```text
Every Monday, 10am
Check afix.sg for pricing/service changes
Alert if something changed
Do not auto-update customer-facing bot knowledge without review
```

This is safer than letting the bot silently change prices based on any website edit.

## What worked well

The setup proved that a small business can get a useful WhatsApp enquiry responder running quickly.

The bot can already handle common messages like:

```text
How much for 3 aircons?
```

Expected answer:

```text
Hi, I'm Mr Afix 👋
Normal servicing for 3 units is $25/unit, total $75.
May I check your location and preferred date/time?
```

It can also handle basic service questions, collect customer details, and route complex issues to the team.

## What still needs improvement

Before using this heavily with real customers, we should improve:

1. **Always-on hosting**  
   Move from a sleeping PC to an always-on mini PC, VPS, or official API.

2. **Human takeover logic**  
   If staff replies manually, pause the bot in that chat.

3. **Cleaner testing across many examples**  
   Test more real customer phrasing, especially Singlish/short-form messages.

4. **Better handling of ambiguous service words**  
   Example: "wash" should default to normal servicing, not chemical wash.

5. **Approved media library**  
   If customers ask for photos of past work, the bot should only send approved Afix-owned images, not random web images.

6. **Conversation review loop**  
   Review the first 20–50 conversations and tighten the prompt based on real mistakes.

## Main learning

The AI is not the hard part.

The hard part is the operating system around the AI:

- what it is allowed to say,
- what it must never say,
- when it should stop,
- how to keep backend noise out of customer chats,
- how to avoid wrong assumptions from casual customer wording,
- how to keep pricing current,
- how to let humans take over cleanly.

For Afix, the right role is simple:

> AI handles first-line enquiries. Humans confirm bookings, discounts, complaints, and custom quotes.

That is enough for now. Do not overbuild before real customer testing.

## Next actions

- Restart Hermes Gateway after every config change.
- Test with 20 realistic WhatsApp messages.
- Save bad replies and correct the rules.
- Move hosting to an always-on machine before relying on it 24/7.
- Add human takeover/pause logic if real chats show the bot interrupting staff.

Aircon leaking, can help?

Can come today?

Each one tests a different failure mode.

The goal is not to make the bot sound clever. The goal is to make it useful, safe, and clean enough that customers feel they are talking to Afix — not to software.
